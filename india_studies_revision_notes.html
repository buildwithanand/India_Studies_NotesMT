Traditional Knowledge (TK)

Unit 1
📌 Definition (WIPO)
TK refers to knowledge, innovations, and practices of indigenous and local communities — developed over centuries, adapted to local culture and environment, collectively owned, and transmitted orally from generation to generation.

What TK Includes

  • Medicinal, Agricultural & Ecological Knowledge
  • Music, Dance, Stories & Poetry
  • Handicrafts (Artesanat)
  • Spiritual Expression
  • Literary, artistic or scientific works; performances; inventions; scientific discoveries; designs; marks, names, symbols

Characteristics of Traditional Knowledge

  • Dynamic, methodical, and universal in nature
  • Transmitted verbally, not in writing
  • Holistic — cannot be compartmentalised, inseparable from the people
  • Based on observational experience → useful for decision-making
  • Associated with sustainable use of native resources
  • Acts as an authority/governance system (respect, obligation to share)

TK vs. Scientific Knowledge

Truth statusAssumed to be the truthBest approximation
DomainSacred and secularSecular only
TeachingStorytelling, learning by doingDidactic, formal education
MediumOral / VisualWritten

Challenges to TK Preservation

  • Lack of adequate management & documentation
  • Knowledge is eroding — elders die, knowledge is lost
  • Competition from other cultures captures young people's imagination
  • Commercialisation and economic instability destroy TK

Indigenous Knowledge (IK)

📖 Definition
Johnson (1992): "A body of knowledge built up by a group of people through generations of living in close contact with nature."

UN Definition of "Indigenous": Groups whose social, cultural and economic conditions distinguish them from other sections of national communities, and whose status is regulated by their own customs, traditions or special laws/regulations.

IK vs. Western Knowledge

Indigenous Knowledge
  • Visual/oral representation
  • Learning by doing & experiencing
  • Holistic approach
  • Subjective
  • Knowledge through storytelling
  • Community-based
Western Knowledge
  • Based on written words
  • Through formal education
  • Reductionist approach
  • Objective
  • Didactic approach
  • Individual-based

Significance of IK

  • Survival of humans depends on wholeness of nature
  • Describes circumstances of communities in connection to the environment
  • Explains cultural behaviours from human adaptation to environment
  • A mix of applied education, economics, politics, religion, sciences, and technologies

Importance

  • Local empowerment, development, self-sufficiency, self-determination
  • Valuable information on managing local environment
  • Provides problem-solving solutions especially for marginalised communities
  • Important component of global knowledge on development challenges

Culture & Civilization

📖 Key Definitions
Culture (Edward B. Tylor, 1871, Primitive Culture): "That complex whole which includes knowledge, belief, art, law, morals, custom, and any other capabilities and habits acquired by man as a member of society."

Etymology: from Latin cult or cultus = tilling, cultivating, refining and worship. Sanskrit equivalent: Sanskriti.

Types of Culture

📦 Material Culture

Dress, food, household goods, technologies, instruments, material goods, modes of production, trade, commerce, architecture. Changes QUICKLY.

💡 Non-Material Culture

Ideas, ideals, thoughts, beliefs, norms, values, myths, legends, literature, ritual, art forms. Changes SLOWLY.

⚡ Cultural Lag — William F. Ogburn
The gap/imbalance in the rate of change between material and non-material culture. Material culture changes fast; non-material is slow to respond → creating a "lag". Example: Indians adopt western technology but retain traditional beliefs.

Elements of Culture (H.M. Johnson)

  • Cognitive Elements — Knowledge about physical and social world
  • Beliefs — Empirically neither true nor false (e.g., Ganga water sacred; Sikhs wear bangles)
  • Values & Norms — Measures of goodness, standards of behaviour; socially produced
  • Symbols — Flags, anthem, statues, gestures
  • Language — Vehicle of culture; entrance into a culture

Characteristics of Culture

  • Social — product of society, not individual
  • Learned behaviour — not biologically inherited
  • Transmitted — parent to child via language
  • Gratifying — satisfies biological and social needs
  • Varies — every culture is unique to its society
  • Continuous & Cumulative — memory of human race, always adding
  • Dynamic — changes but not static

Functions of Culture

  • Treasury of knowledge | Defines situations | Defines attitude, values, goals
  • Decides our career | Provides behaviour pattern | Moulds personality

Civilization vs. Culture

Etymology: "Civilization" from Latin Civitas = city. Goldenweiser used both terms identically. MacIver & Page: "Civilization is the whole apparatus of life."
CivilizationCulture
Has a precise standard of measurementCannot be precisely measured
Always advancing (technical achievement improves past)Not necessarily advancing (Gautama Buddha not surpassed)
More easily communicated (e.g., radio)Less easily communicated (e.g., poetic talent)
Borrowed without loss/change (technical devices)Difficult to borrow intact (foreigner following Indian culture)
External (material wealth)Internal (intrinsic values)

Cultural Heritage

  • Culture inherited from predecessors = Cultural Heritage
  • Levels: Human heritage → National heritage
  • Ministry of Culture preserves & promotes cultural heritage
  • Network: 6 attached offices + 2 subordinate offices + 33 autonomous organisations
  • Key bodies: National Museum, National Gallery of Modern Art, Archaeological Survey of India, National Archives of India, Anthropological Survey of India

Science & Technology in Ancient India

FieldKey Facts
MathematicsGanita = broad word. Zero discovered in India (2nd century BC). First text mentioning zero = Brahmagupta's Brahmasputa Siddhanta. Aryabhatta = algebra, area of triangle → trigonometry. Sulvasutras = source of Indian maths.
AstronomyJyotishvedanga texts → systematic astronomy. Aryabhatta (499 AD) — marked shift from astrology to science. Planetary motion emphasised.
MedicineTakshila & Varanasi as hubs. Charaksamhita (Charak) — plants and herbs. Sushrutasamhita (Sushruta) — surgery as separate stream (~4th century AD). Work spread to China and Central Asia.
MetallurgyBy 1st century AD: iron, copper, silver, gold, brass, bronze. Iron Pillar (Qutub Minar complex) — exceptional alloying. Dyeing textiles: quality seen in Ajanta frescoes.
GeographyPeople studied geography due to ongoing interplay with environment

  • Pattin Ganita — calculations with numerals
  • Rekha Ganita — Geometry (line works)

Constitution of India — Introduction & Preamble

Unit 1
⚡ Quick Stats
• Came into force: 26 January 1950 | Adopted: 26 November 1949
• Original: 395 Articles, 8 Schedules, 22 Parts
• Current (2021): ~448 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules
• Time to draft: 2 years, 11 months, 18 days
• Constituent Assembly first met: 9 Dec 1946 | President: Dr. Rajendra Prasad | Vice-President: H.C. Mukerjee

Preamble

We, the people of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a Sovereign, Socialist Secular Democratic and Republic and to secure to its citizens: Justice, Social, Economic and Political; Liberty of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship; Equality of status and opportunity; and to promote among them all Fraternity assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and the integrity of the Nation. In our Constituent Assembly this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949 do hereby, Adopt, Enact and Give to Ourselves this Constitution. — Preamble to the Constitution of India

Nature of Indian State — S.S.S.D.R.

1Sovereign
Supreme power, independent, absolute and uncontrolled — internally supreme and externally free.
2Socialist
Added by 42nd Amendment Act, 1976. Aims at elimination of inequality in income, status and standards of life. India follows "Democratic Socialism" (mixed economy — both public and private sectors), NOT Communistic Socialism.
3Secular
Added by 42nd Amendment Act, 1976. State does not recognise any religion. All religions treated equally. S.R. Bommai v. Union of India — SC held "secularism is basic feature of the Constitution." Articles 25–28 guarantee religious freedom.
4Democratic
Demo = People; Cracy = Rule. Authority from will of the people. Direct (Switzerland — Referendum, Initiative, Recall, Plebiscite) vs Indirect (India).
5Republic
Head of state is NOT a hereditary monarch. India has an elected President.

Objectives of Preamble (JELF)

  • Justice — Social, Economic and Political
  • Equality — of status and opportunity
  • Liberty — of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship
  • Fraternity — assuring dignity of individual and unity and integrity of the nation

Preamble — Key Facts

  • Objective Resolution moved by Nehru on 13 Dec 1946, adopted 22 Jan 1947
  • Words "Socialist," "Secular," and "Integrity" added by 42nd Amendment, 1976
  • Preamble does NOT grant any power; gives structure on which Constitution stands
  • Dr. Ambedkar: "Key to open the minds of the makers"

Fundamental Rights — Part III (Art 12–35)

Part III is the "Magna Carta of India." Originally 7 rights; Right to Property (Art 31) removed by 44th Amendment, 1978 — now a legal right under Art 300A.

6 Fundamental Rights at a Glance

RightArticlesKey Points
Right to Equality14–18Equality before law (British) + Equal protection (US); Reasonable classification allowed; Art 17 = Untouchability abolished
Right to Freedom19–226 freedoms under Art 19; Art 20 = 3 protections (ex-post-facto, double jeopardy, self-incrimination); Art 21 = Life & Liberty; Art 21A = Education; Art 22 = Arrest protection
Right Against Exploitation23–24Art 23 = prohibition of human trafficking & forced labour; Art 24 = no child labour in factories (below 14 years)
Right to Freedom of Religion25–28Freedom of conscience, to profess, practice, propagate religion subject to public order, morality, health

Right to Constitutional Remedies32"Heart and Soul of the Constitution" per Dr. Ambedkar; 5 writs; SC = protector of FR

Articles 12–13 (Definitions)

Art 12Definition of "State"
Includes: (1) Government and Parliament of India (2) Government and legislature of State (3) Local authorities (municipalities, district boards, village panchayats) (4) Other authorities (LIC, ONGC, SAIL, etc.) — even private bodies acting as instruments of State.
Art 13Laws inconsistent with FR
  • Art 13(1) = Pre-Constitutional laws inconsistent with FR → void
  • Art 13(2) = State shall NOT make laws abridging FR → void if made
  • Art 13(3) = "Law" includes ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom
  • Doctrines: Doctrine of Severability | Doctrine of Eclipse | Judicial Review

Right to Equality (Art 14–18)

Art 14Equality Before Law + Equal Protection
Equality before law = English/Dicey's Rule of Law = Negative concept (no discrimination).
Equal protection = US origin = Positive concept (reasonable classification permitted).
Exceptions: Art 361 (President/Governor); Armed forces; Foreign Sovereigns, Ambassadors.
Reasonable Classification: Geographical basis; State favouring itself (Jallikattu); Taxation; Special courts.
Art 15Prohibition of Discrimination
No discrimination on grounds of: religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth.
Exceptions:
  • Art 15(3) — Special provisions for women and children (1st Amendment 1951)
  • Art 15(4) — SC/ST/OBCs educational advancement (1st Amendment 1951)
  • Art 15(5) — Private educational institutions (93rd Amendment 2005)
  • Art 15(6) — EWS = max 10% (103rd Amendment 2019)
Art 16Equality in Public Employment
Art 16(1) = Equality of opportunity in public employment.
Art 16(2) = No discrimination on religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth, residence.
Exceptions: Residence condition for certain posts; Reservation for backward classes; SC/ST reservation; EWS max 10% (103rd Amendment 2019).
Art 17Abolition of Untouchability
Practice of untouchability is an offence punishable by law.
Art 18Abolition of Titles
No title (except military/academic) shall be conferred by the State. No citizen shall accept any title from a foreign State.

Art 20Protection in Conviction for Offences
  • 20(1) Ex post facto laws — Cannot be convicted for act not an offence when committed; penalty cannot exceed what was prescribed
  • 20(2) Double Jeopardy — "Nemo debet vis vexari" — no man put twice in peril for same offence
  • 20(3) Self-Incrimination — No person shall be compelled to be a witness against himself. Narco-analysis/brain-mapping/lie detector without consent = violates Art 20(3) [Selvi v. State of Karnataka]
Art 21Right to Life & Personal Liberty
"No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law."

Key Judgment — Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (AIR 1978 SC 597): Golden Triangle Test (Art 21 + 14 + 19); "Procedure established by law" must be just, fair and reasonable; Right to travel abroad included.

Sub-Rights under Art 21: Right to privacy; Right to education; Right to speedy trial; Right to bail; Right to humane treatment in prison; Right against torture; Right to live with dignity; Right to compensation; Right to clean environment; etc.
Art 21ARight to Education
Inserted by 86th Amendment Act, 2002. Free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14 years.
Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009 — enforced from 1 April 2010.
Art 51A(k) also added by 86th Amendment (fundamental duty for education).
Art 22Protection Against Arrest & Detention
Right to be informed of grounds of arrest; Right to consult and be defended by a lawyer; Produced before magistrate within 24 hours; Cannot be detained beyond 24 hours without magistrate's order.

Art 25–28: Right to Freedom of Religion

Art 25Freedom of Conscience & Religious Freedom
All persons equally entitled to freedom of conscience and right to freely profess, practice, and propagate religion — subject to public order, morality, health.
Not absolute: No human sacrifice; No worship on busy highways; Devadasi system restricted; Loudspeaker/fireworks regulated by SC.
Art 26Freedom to Manage Religious Affairs
Right to establish and maintain institutions for religious purposes; manage own affairs in religious matters.
Art 27Freedom from Taxes for Religion
No person shall be compelled to pay taxes for promotion of any particular religion.
Art 28Freedom from Religious Instruction
No religious instruction in wholly State-funded educational institutions.

Cultural & Educational Rights (Art 29–30)

Art 29Protection of Interests of Minorities
Section of citizens with distinct language, script, or culture has the right to conserve the same. No citizen shall be denied admission to State-maintained institution on grounds of religion, race, caste, language.
Art 30Right of Minorities to Establish Educational Institutions
All minorities (religious or linguistic) shall have the right to establish and administer educational institutions. State shall not discriminate in granting aid on grounds of minority management.

Writs — Art 32 & Art 226

Art 32 — Right to move Supreme Court for enforcement of FRs. Dr. Ambedkar: "If I was asked to name any particular Article as the most important... I could not refer to any other Article except this one. It is the very soul of the Constitution."
Art 226 — High Courts also empowered to issue writs.
Habeas Corpus
"You may have the body / Produce the body"
Most important writ for personal liberty. Order to set free a person arbitrarily arrested/detained. Used when kept in jail without legal justification.
Mandamus
"We order"
Issued against public authority under a legal duty to do/forbear something. Used for public purposes to enforce performance of public duties.
Prohibition
"Prevent / Prohibit"
Preventive in nature ("Prevention is better than cure"). Prevents inferior court from exceeding jurisdiction BEFORE passing order. Against judicial/quasi-judicial bodies.
Certiorari
"To certify"
Remedial writ — quashes order already made without jurisdiction or in violation of natural justice. Issued AFTER order is passed. Against judicial/quasi-judicial bodies.
Quo Warranto
"By what authority"
Prevents person who wrongfully usurped a public office from continuing in it. Protects FRs from govt orders based on favouritism.
Prohibition vs Certiorari: Both lie against judicial/quasi-judicial bodies (NOT executive). Prohibition = before order; Certiorari = after order. They may sometimes overlap.

Key Facts — CM

  • Appointment: Leader of majority party in State Legislative Assembly; Personal discretion if no clear majority
  • Tenure: Not fixed; holds office at pleasure of Governor; Cannot be dismissed if enjoying majority
  • Death/Resignation/Dismissal of CM dissolves the entire CoM

Collective vs Individual Responsibility

Collective Responsibility
  • All ministers jointly responsible for acts of commission/omission
  • "Swim together or sink together"
  • No-confidence motion → all must resign
  • Cabinet decisions bind ALL ministers
Individual Responsibility
  • Implicit in the Doctrine of Pleasure (Art 164)
  • Minister who lost confidence of CM can be removed
  • Wrongdoing or difference of opinion → CM can ask to resign

Classification of Ministers

  • Cabinet Ministers — Important ministries (Home, Defence, Finance, External Affairs); attend Cabinet meetings; policy-making
  • Ministers of State (MoS) — Independent charge OR attached to Cabinet Minister; do NOT attend Cabinet meetings unless specially invited
  • Deputy Ministers — No independent charge; attached to Cabinet Ministers or MoS; not part of Cabinet

Cabinet

  • Smaller body: 20–30 ministers (Cabinet Ministers only)
  • Meets frequently (usually ≥ once a week)
  • Policy decisions binding on all ministers; supervisory role over CoM

Kitchen Cabinet

  • Super-small body with highest decision-making powers
  • Informal and extra-constitutional; developed to ensure quick, efficient decisions
  • Helps maintain secrecy in important political matters; also exists in USA and Britain

State Legislature (Art 168–212)

States with Bicameral Legislatures: Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Karnataka (6 states). All others = Unicameral.
🏛️ Legislative Assembly (Lower House)

Members: Directly elected by people (universal adult franchise). Max = 500, Min = 60. Exceptions: Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Goa = 30; Mizoram = 40; Nagaland = 46.
Term: 5 years (auto-dissolves on expiration)

🏛️ Legislative Council (Upper House)

Members: Indirectly elected. Max = 1/3 of Assembly, Min = 40.
Nature: Permanent body (NOT subject to dissolution). 1/3 retire every 2 years; member serves for 6 years.

Composition of Legislative Council (Manner of Election)

  • 1/3 — Elected by members of local bodies (municipalities, district boards, etc.)
  • 1/12 — Elected by graduates of 3 years standing in the state
  • 1/12 — Elected by teachers of 3 years standing (secondary school level or above)
  • 1/3 — Elected by members of Legislative Assembly (from non-assembly members)
  • Remainder — Nominated by Governor (literature, science, art, co-operative movement, social service)
  • Total: 5/6 indirectly elected + 1/6 nominated by Governor

Qualifications for State Legislature

  • Citizen of India
  • Age: ≥30 for Legislative Council; ≥25 for Legislative Assembly
  • Must take oath; elector for assembly constituency in the concerned state

Disqualifications

  • Holds any Government office of profit
  • Declared unsound mind by competent court
  • Undischarged insolvent
  • Not a citizen of India or voluntarily acquired foreign citizenship
  • Under any law made by Parliament
  • Anti-defection law (Tenth Schedule) — decided by Speaker/Chairman; Subject to Judicial Review (Kihota Hollohan v. Zachilhu, 1992)

Union Executive Components

  • President (Art 52 — Head of State)
  • Vice President | Prime Minister | Council of Ministers | Attorney General of India

PM Appointment & Tenure

  • Leader of majority party in Lok Sabha; personal discretion if no clear majority
  • PM can be member of Lok Sabha OR Rajya Sabha (unlike Britain — House of Commons requirement)
  • Tenure: not fixed; holds office during pleasure of President
  • Cannot be dismissed if enjoying majority in Lok Sabha

Master Case Law List

MUST KNOW
S.R. Bommai v. Union of India
Held: "Secularism is the basic feature of the Constitution." (Art 356 — President's Rule)
A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras (AIR 1950 SC 27)
Held: Narrow interpretation of "personal liberty" (Art 21) — merely liberty of physical body. Articles 19 and 21 independent. "Procedure established by law" ≠ "Due process"
Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (AIR 1978 SC 597)
Held: Broadened "personal liberty." Art 21 + 14 + 19 — Golden Triangle Test. Procedure must be just, fair and reasonable. Right to travel abroad is part of personal liberty. "Law" includes rules of natural justice.
Francis Coralie Mullin v. Union Territory of Delhi (AIR 1981 SC 746)
Held: Right to life includes right to live with human dignity.
Mohini Jain v. State of Karnataka (AIR 1992 SC 1858)
Held: Charging capitation fee is illegal; right to education flows from Art 21 (all ages).
Unnikrishnan v. State of AP (1993) 1 SC 645
Held: Right to free and compulsory education only for 6–14 years age group (not all ages). Led to 86th Amendment Act, 2002.
Kedarnath v. State of Bihar
Subject: Sec 124A of IPC (Sedition Law) — Art 19(1)(a) and Free Speech
Kedarnath v. State of West Bengal (AIR 1953 SC 404)
Subject: Ex-post-facto law (Art 20(1)) — Enhancement of punishment does not apply retrospectively.
Maqbool Hussain v. State of Bombay
Held: Customs authority is not judicial in nature; Art 20(2) — Double Jeopardy does not apply to customs proceedings.
Nandini Satpathy v. PL Dani
Held: Accused has right to silence during interrogation if answer would incriminate. Art 20(3) — self-incrimination protection.
Selvi v. State of Karnataka
Held: Compulsory Narco-analysis, brain-mapping, and polygraph tests without consent = unconstitutional, violate right to privacy and Art 20(3).
Shreya Singal v. Union of India
Subject: Struck down Sec 66A of IT Act — violation of Art 19(1)(a) Free Speech.
Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India (2020)
Held: Right to internet is a fundamental right under Art 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g). Freedom of press was not violated as circulation was permitted.
Balaji v. State of Mysore (AIR 1960 Mys 338)
Held: Caste alone cannot be the sole test to ascertain backwardness (Art 16).
Preeti Sagar Srivastava v. State of Madhya Pradesh (AIR 1999 SC 2894)
Held (4:1): Merit alone can be the criterion for selecting students for medical and engineering courses (Art 16).
Resham v. State of Karnataka (Hijab Case)
Held: Hijab is not an essential religious practice in Islam. School uniform is not violative of fundamental rights. Hijab ban GO of 5 Feb 2022 upheld (Karnataka HC, Full Bench).
University of Mysore v. Govinda Rao (AIR 1965 SC 491)
Subject: Writ of Quo Warranto — prevents wrongful usurpation of public office.
Kihota Hollohan v. Zachilhu (1992)
Held: Decision of Speaker under Anti-Defection Law (10th Schedule) is subject to Judicial Review.
AIIMS Students Union v. AIIMS
Subject: Related to Fundamental Duties (Art 51A)
Sarbananda Sonowal v. Union of India
Subject: Related to Fundamental Duties — protection of sovereignty and integrity (Art 51A(c))
Bombay Hawkers Union v. Bombay Municipal Corporation (AIR 1985 SC 1206)
Subject: Art 19(1)(g) — Street hawkers' right to carry on trade; reasonable restriction permitted.
